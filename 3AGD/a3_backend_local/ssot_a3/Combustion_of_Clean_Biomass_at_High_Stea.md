ABSTRACT: Biomass fuel exists in various forms, traditionally as wood, bark, harvesting residues sewage sludge
and organic waste resulting from agricultural industry. With the actual on or near site availability of biomass fuels
and the trend for independent power production the demand for reliable biomass fired power plants is increasing. To
satisfy this demand, a combustion technology, which can cope with a broad fuel range as well as with low ash
melting points, and is able to produce steam at high steam parameters without the risk of corrosion on the other hand,
is necessary. To comply with these demands the standard bubbling fluidized bed technology normally operating in
combustion mode was modified to a staged combustion concept. The first FBC-plant with such high steam
parameters was successfully put in operation last year in Umeå/Sweden. The boiler, which is designed for the
combustion of a mixture of wood fuels, peat Salix and energy crops, has a thermal capacity of 120 MW which

143 bar and 543 °C is not only used for electric power production but also for district heat generation. The whole

cleaning.

4 OPERATION RESULTS
The commissioning started in October 2008 and the
plant was handed over to the client after 6 weeks of
successful trial run in January 2010. Reason for the late
PAC was the planned shut-down of the boiler during the
warm months of the year between June and September.
Some delays occurred during commissioning because
of the strong winter. Actually the turbine (not in scope of
AE&E) was not delivered on schedule because the cargo
ship was frozen in the river Elbe.
The distribution of the solid fuel in the daily bins was
not satisfying because of the usage of rotating
distribution chutes on top of the bins and a wrong
dropping angle of the solid fuel. As a result a subsidence
slope of approx. ± 60% occurred inside of the bins
especially when operating at high levels. Besides that the
weight of the solid fuel stored in the bin was too much (at
high levels) for the screw reclaimer to operate. To solve
the problem the normal operation level was set down to
40%, which led to a safe and satisfying continuous
operation.

6 CONCLUSION
The ECOFLUID© technology is capable to combust
biomass fuels within a broad operation range securely at
high steam parameters. Low stable part load operation
can be achieved as well as high superheating
temperatures of 540°C without the risk of corrosion. The
operating experiences from the plant in Umeå were the
basis for the further development of the technology. The
new boiler concept comprises a compact solution which
provides the basis to convert biogenic fuels to steam and
finally electricity with the benefit of reduces investment
costs. This is a major contribution to enhance green
power production on an environmentally friendly basis.
