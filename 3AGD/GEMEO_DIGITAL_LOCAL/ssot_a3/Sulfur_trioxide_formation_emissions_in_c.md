3

3

2 3 2 3

3

2
concentrations, as noted byAhn et al.125 in the pilot-scale tests with PRB coal (22.2% CaO in

3

4.5 ModellingStudies of SO Formation
3
Only a few numerical studies on SO formation are available in the open literature for
3
oxy-ascomparedtoair-combustion.Burdettetal.81simulatedtheeffectofoperatingbed
pressure on SO -to-SO conversion for air-combustion. The predictions for bed pressure
2 3
(600 kPa) and temperature of 900°C with 10% of O in the flue gas clearly indicated
2
increasing SO formation with increasing oxygen formation. This work suggests that
3
under such conditions the potential conversion of sulfur-in-fuel to SO could be as high
3
as 20%. Fleig et al.126 modelled homogeneous gas-phase SO formation for oxy-fuel
3
combustion conditions with a model which is described by Giménez-Lopez et al.79 and
which was initiallydeveloped by Glarborg et al.153 CHEMKIN-PRO was used to model
homogeneous reactions considering plug flow reactor. More recently, Belo et al.89
implemented a kinetic model of Burdett et al.81 for residence time of 0.3 to 0.9 s (Table
6).Duetothermodynamicconsiderations,themaximumconversionofSO toSO wasat
2 3
low temperatures and further increases of temperature to 900 and 1000ºC decreased the
SO by ~6.8 and ~3.2%, respectively.89 SO formation was also modelled by Schneider
3 3
andBogdan,154 foranoil-firedboiler.
5. Research Needs
A significant amount of effort has been made to evaluate SO concentrations from
3
laboratory-scaletocommercial-scaleunits.However,oxy-fuelcombustionrequiresmore
attention in terms of better understanding SO formation mechanisms. At this point the
3
majority of SO measurements have been conducted in conventional PC and gas-fired
3
combustion systems, while onlya few studies are available for oxy-fired FB combustors
andpilot-scalestudies arestill sparse.Majorresearchshouldconcentrateontheeffect of
FGR andcatalyticconversion.SO formationbothdependongases suchas NO,COand
3
their effects may be different in oxy-fuel combustion and, hence more experiments are
neededonSO formationwithdifferenttypesofcoals.DevelopmentofpressurizedFBC
3
systems would also require SO formation tests as experimental data on the effect of
3
pressure are rare.
Inthecaseofanalyticalmethods,allthecurrentpost-combustionSO measurementsstill
3
have technical challenges associated mainly with temperature control as SO is a very
3
34

reactivemoleculeandSO caneasilybelostinthesamplingline.Atpresent,CCMisthe
3
most used and reliable technique to quantify SO concentrations, while other methods
3
suchas thesalt methodorcalcium oxalatewithglass beads needtobeimproved.
6. Conclusions
Thepresent paper reviews theformationSO incoal combustionsystems, andespecially
3
oxy-fuel technology.The followingconclusions canbedrawn:
- SO concentration in oxy-fuel combustion is typically several times higher than
3
that in air combustion. The higher level of SO is mainly due to the increased
3
concentrations ofO ,H OandSO intheboiler
2 2 2
- Wet flue gas recycling is associated with higher SO concentration than dry flue
3
gas recycling. An increased amount of H O promotes SO formation apparently
2 3
due to the production of OH radicals. However, H O concentrations higher than
2
15% in the system strongly inhibit SO formation. Inhibition appears to be more
3
pronouncedinactual PC tests thaninsimulatedfluegas tests.
- Higher conversion of S to SO occurs near the burnout region where O is found
3 2
inexcessamounts. Inthisregion,gas-phasehomogeneousformationisdominant
forSO formation.
3
- ThepresenceofgasessuchasNOandCOmayenhanceSO formation.However,
3
these effects appear to be small compared to other factors such as combustion
temperature,excess O andpresenceofcatalysts.
2
- SO formationisenhancedduetocatalyticeffectofflyash.Higherconcentrations
3
of Fe O and V O and other metal oxides increase SO concentrations
2 3 2 5 3
significantly. Adsorption of SO by alkali and alkaline earthmetal oxides is also
3
animportant phenomenon.
- The CCM method appears to be the most dependable technique for measuring
SO concentrations.
