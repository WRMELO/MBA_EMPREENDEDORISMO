Discussion
In order to understand the boiler dynamics and recognise factors which affect the process dynamics,
it is important to carry out and analyse diverse process experiments. The dynamic behaviour when
using different fuels and fuel mixtures and their combustion in two different sized reactors is
compared. Study of the steady-state combustion reveals odd features concerning the co-combustion
process.
SUMMARY PASSAGE 4
Steady-State Analyses
The burning regimes model [31] has been presented earlier and was used to analyse combustion
regimes of coal, wood chips and mixtures of these fuels. The operation of the model is based on the
idea that a burning fuel particle initially loses its mass due to drying and devolatilization, i.e. its
average density decreases, but its diameter remains approximately constant. The analysis was
carried out for different size fractions and summed up to find the locations of the release of volatiles
and char combustion.
SUMMARY PASSAGE 5
Burning In Bed Region
The moisture content of wood is substantially higher than that of coals. Coal with higher density
contains a great amount of char and combustion occurs mainly in the bed area. Wood with a lower
density does not stay in the bed but releases moisture there on route to the freeboard.
SUMMARY PASSAGE 6
Burning In Circulation
As discussed, wood chips release moisture in the bed area, yet wood devolatilization also occurs,
which consumes oxygen from the bed. The measured oxygen profiles and the result from the burning
regimes model, shown in Figs. 8 and 3, support this conclusion. Low temperature and oxygen
concentration in the bed slow the combustion of the coal char considerably which increases char
inventory in the bed.

SUMMARY PASSAGE 9
Conclusions
Measurements with coal, wood chips and mixture of coal and wood chips showed that temperature
profiles with burning of mixtures cannot be deduced directly from the burning profiles of individual fuel
components. The emphasis of the co-firing trials was on the dynamic changes. Also the dynamic
responses for mixtures can be surprisingly different from the responses when burning of individual
fuels.
